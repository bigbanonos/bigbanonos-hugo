One year ago tonight, David Jones Garcia played 19 minutes for the San Antonio Spurs and did a little of everything.

Twelve points on 5-of-7 shooting. Six assists. Five rebounds. Three steals. San Antonio beat Atlanta 135-126.

It is still his career high. He turns 25 on Tuesday.

## Who is David Jones Garcia?

He is from Guachupita, a neighborhood in Santo Domingo, born Nov. 24, 2001. He came to the U.S. for high school at Teays Valley Christian in West Virginia, played college ball at DePaul, St. John's and Memphis, where he went by David Jones, and went undrafted in 2024.

The route since then:

* **2024-25:** A two-way contract with Utah, then G League stints in Salt Lake City and Mexico City, where he averaged [24.5 points and 7.5 rebounds over 31 games](https://gleague.nba.com/news/spurs-sign-david-jones-garcia-to-two-way-contract).
* **Summer 2025:** All-Summer League First Team for the Spurs, shooting better than 50 percent from three. San Antonio signed him to a two-way deal on July 23.
* **Oct. 22, 2025:** NBA debut at Dallas. Five points in four minutes, in a 33-point win. Dominican press counted him as [the 13th Dominican on an NBA roster](https://elnuevodiario.com.do/jones-debuta-en-la-nba-con-el-pie-derecho-en-la-victoria-de-los-spurs/).
* **January 2026:** Picked as one of seven G League players for the Rising Stars game at All-Star weekend.
* **February 2026:** Ankle surgery. Season over.
* **July 2026:** Re-signed on another two-way with San Antonio, [despite interest from Olympiacos](https://www.eurohoops.net/en/nba-news/1993346/spurs-to-keep-david-jones-garcia-despite-olympiacos-links/).

He has played four NBA games this season, mostly late minutes in wins. Most of his work will come with the Austin Spurs in the G League.

## The Dominican Republic beat Team USA. Again.

On Feb. 26, 2026, in Oceanside, California, the Dominican Republic [beat the United States 87-79](https://www.fiba.basketball/en/events/fiba-basketball-world-cup-2027-americas-qualifiers/news/dominican-republic-confirm-status-as-road-warriors-with-fourth-straight-away-win) in World Cup qualifying. Jassel Pérez had 20 points and 10 rebounds. Five players scored in double figures.

They did it without Karl-Anthony Towns. They did it without Jones Garcia.

Before that night, the Dominican Republic had beaten the United States once, [116-108 at the 1989 AmeriCup](https://about.fiba.basketball/es/news/world-cup-americas-qualifiers-five-must-watch-games).

That is the best game in the country's basketball history, and it is not close.

## Where the Dominican Republic stands now

After a 101-66 win over Chile in September, the Dominicans were 6-2 in Group E of the Americas qualifiers, second behind the United States. The top three teams in each group go to the 2027 World Cup in Qatar.

It would be the country's fifth World Cup: Philippines 1978, then Spain 2014, China 2019 and the 2023 edition in Asia.

The remaining group games include a home-and-away pair with Colombia. FIBA had not posted dates or times when this was published. Check [the Dominican Republic team page on FIBA.basketball](https://www.fiba.basketball/en/events/fiba-basketball-world-cup-2027-americas-qualifiers/teams/dominican-republic).

## The Dominican Republic in FIBA play

The AmeriCup is the tournament the Dominican Republic plays for most often: 15 appearances, one medal.

| Year | Event | Finish |
|---|---|---|
| 1978 | World Championship (Philippines) | 12th of 14 |
| 1989 | Americas Championship | 6th, with the first-ever win over the USA |
| 2011 | Americas Championship | **Bronze**, coached by John Calipari |
| 2012 | Olympic Qualifying Tournament | 4th, one spot short of London |
| 2013 | AmeriCup | 4th |
| 2014 | World Cup (Spain) | 13th of 24 |
| 2019 | World Cup (China) | 16th of 32 |
| 2023 | World Cup (Philippines, Japan, Indonesia) | 14th of 32 |
| 2025 | AmeriCup (Nicaragua) | 5th |

In the Caribbean regional tournament, Centrobasket, the Dominicans have won gold three times: 1977, 2004 and 2012. They have never played in the Olympics.

## How the Dominican league works

The Liga Nacional de Baloncesto, now branded La Súper Liga LNB, is a spring-and-summer league. The 2026 season, the league's 20th, tipped off April 14 and wrapped in July, early this year to make room for the Central American and Caribbean Games.

Gigantes Basketball Club of San Francisco de Macorís won the title, beating Metros de Santiago 91-80 in Game 6 of the final. Charles Brown Jr. was finals MVP.

**How to watch:** Every LNB game streams free on YouTube at [@LNBRDTV](https://www.youtube.com/@LNBRDTV). The 2026 final drew more than 758,000 views.

The 2027 season should start next spring.
